Do Trading Signals Beat Buy-and-Hold? A Backtest and a Live Alerting Pipeline

Research
Python
pandas
NumPy
SciPy
An eight-strategy, 19-year backtest that tested whether the technical signals from a self-built Raspberry Pi alerting pipeline were worth trading. They were not, and a published trend filter cut drawdown by more than half at a cost of about 1 point of annual return.

Code

The problem

A long-horizon, tax-sheltered account must choose between passive buy-and-hold and tactical rules (trend filters, momentum, oscillator signals). Popular signals are widely promoted, but whether they improve risk-adjusted outcomes after costs is an empirical question.

Approach

A vectorized daily engine applies each strategy’s target weights to adjusted ETF prices (SPY, EFA, IEF, GSG, VNQ, AGG, with BIL as cash) from 2007-05-30 to 2026-05-08. All strategies share the same dates, cost model (1 bp per side) and cash proxy. Results are CAGR, volatility, Sharpe, Sortino, max drawdown, Calmar and turnover against buy-and-hold SPY. The signals come from HedgeAbove, a cron-driven rule engine on a Raspberry Pi with SQLite dedup, Telegram delivery, point-in-time SEC EDGAR fundamentals and walk-forward threshold selection.

NoteWhy it matters for a decision

Frames an allocation choice as decision analysis under uncertainty: state the criterion (drawdown and risk-adjusted return, not raw CAGR), test the alternatives on equal terms, and accept a negative result. The self-built signals were kept as monitoring telemetry, not used as a strategy.

Limitations

  • A single 19-year sample dominated by one major bear market (2008-09) and a short one (2020); no 1970s-style inflation regime.
  • Trades fill at the same close that generates the signal. With next-day execution, Faber’s Sharpe falls from 0.65 to 0.59 and its CAGR from 9.59% to 8.80%.
  • Trend and momentum strategies sit in cash during their 200-252 day warm-up (May 2007 to mid-2008), which flatters them. Over a common post-warm-up window the Sharpe edge shrinks from +0.14 to +0.09.
  • Eight strategies were compared without a multiple-testing adjustment.
  • Static weights are effectively rebalanced daily at zero cost. Only 1 bp costs are tested. No slippage model.
  • yfinance adjusted prices are revised after the fact, and the data vintage is not committed.
  • Educational research only, not investment advice.
